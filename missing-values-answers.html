4. Batch size for F and G likely 32

The method suggested in the PDF will not work: progress.json for F and G does not record steps per epoch. Indirect evidence points to 32: Model C was launched by a committed script with no override (so 32), and F's epoch time is within 5% of C's on the same network (236 s against 226 s). At 32 that is 1,520 steps per epoch on 48,645 training images. Shell history on the training PC is the only direct proof.

5. Landmark regressor validation error needs training PC

The weights file and the landmark cache are not on this laptop. A ready script, src/eval_landmark_regressor.py, reports MSE and MAE in normalised units and pixels plus the constant-predictor baseline. It has not been run. Two facts for the manuscript:

  • The regressor was trained only on images where MediaPipe found a hand: 27.45% of the 54,049.
  • Its validation set is the last 10% of those images (Keras validation_split), not a separate seed-42 split.

6. Regressor training time upper bound

Model B's final launch loaded the regressor already trained, so it was trained in one of two earlier short launches (319 s and 526 s, each including about 3 minutes of data loading). That puts it under roughly 6 minutes. Landmark extraction itself took 3,000.6 s.

9. Qualitative description of the visual differences done

From viewing real_vs_generated.png and the five samples_*.png grids:

  • Models A and B (noise only). Outputs are hand-like, with recognisable pose for open-hand letters such as al, bb, la and sheen. Fingers are soft and often merged or missing. Closed-fist letters (fa, gaaf, ha, khaa) collapse into fragmentary dark masses. Backgrounds are uniformly light. Pose varies widely between samples of the same letter. B shows no visible improvement over A.
  • Model C. Outputs follow the reference hand's silhouette with sharp contours. Periodic checkerboard artefacts appear in the background: scattered patches on light backgrounds, and across the whole frame on dark or textured ones (dha, ghain, ha, khaa, laam, thal).
  • Model F. Same structure as C. The artefacts are largely gone on light backgrounds but remain on dark or textured ones.
  • Model G. The cleanest of the five. Only faint residual speckle remains on a few letters (ha, khaa, laam, thal, aleff).
  • C, F and G together. The eight samples per letter are visually near-identical. The noise vector has little visible effect, so variety comes from the structure map, not from the noise.

Additional runs

15–16. Seeded evaluation and full per-class accuracy not completed

The code is written. src/paper_eval.py now seeds everything, trains one classifier, saves it, and prints its path, modification time, seed and samples per class. src/confusion_matrix.py loads that same classifier and writes the full 32-value vector per model to per_class_accuracy.json and .csv.

The run failed twice on this laptop:

  • GPU: this TensorFlow build cannot run convolutions on the RTX 5060.
  • CPU: it trained three epochs normally (validation accuracy 0.967, about 630 s each), then was stopped because the system ran low on memory. Nothing was saved.

A third CPU attempt was stopped the same way after 6 of 12 epochs. Its per-epoch numbers were identical to the earlier attempt, so the seed makes the classifier reproducible. The whole pipeline (train, save, reload in the second script, per-class output) was also checked end to end on a small 80-images-per-class sample; the full-data results are still missing.

What to run on the training PC

bash collect_training_machine_facts.sh
cd src && python -u paper_eval.py | tee ../reports/paper/logs/paper_eval_seed42.log
python -u confusion_matrix.py | tee ../reports/paper/logs/confusion_seed42.log

The first script copies the five histories, summarises Model A's timing and update counts, evaluates the regressor against a constant baseline, records the environment and writes both pip freeze files. It has not been run, because the files it reads are not on this laptop. The two evaluation commands overwrite the existing metrics.json and confusion outputs unless ARASL_EVAL_OUT points to another folder.
