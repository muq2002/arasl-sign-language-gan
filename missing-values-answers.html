3. Model A generator-update count done

2 generator updates per discriminator step in epochs 1–11, and 3 in epochs 12–50 (139 update-epochs in total, i.e. 22 + 117), the same schedule as Model B.

How it was derived: src/train_model_a.py starts at G_UPDATES_BASE = 2 and, after each epoch, uses 3 updates if that epoch's G/D ratio exceeded G_D_RATIO_MAX = 2.0 (4 if it exceeded 3.0). The original gd_ratio values rise from 0.80 to 1.69 over epochs 1–10, reach 2.19 at epoch 11, and stay between 2.16 and 2.20 until epoch 50, so 4 updates were never triggered. The measured epoch time confirms the switch: 401.5 s at epoch 11, then 548.9 s at epoch 12.

Direct count from the final checkpoint (ckpt-50): the generator optimizer applied 234,676 updates and the discriminator 84,409, a ratio of 2.780, exactly the schedule's 139/50 = 2.780. With 1,689 steps per epoch (54,049 images, batch 32) the schedule predicts 234,771 generator updates; the 95 missing ones are steps skipped by mixed-precision loss scaling (inf/NaN gradients), which the optimizer does not count. File: reports/paper/results/training_machine/optimizer_iterations.txt.

4. Batch size for F and G done — 32

Direct proof from the checkpoints. progress.json has no steps-per-epoch field, but each final checkpoint stores how many updates the optimizer applied. All datasets drop the last partial batch, so at batch 32 a model on 48,645 training images makes 1,520 steps per epoch, i.e. 76,000 discriminator updates in 50 epochs:

Batch 16 would give about 152,000 updates and batch 64 about 38,000, so F and G used batch 32 (1,520 steps per epoch). The 29–31 missing updates are steps skipped by mixed-precision loss scaling.

Supporting evidence: the training PC's shell history contains no ARASL_BATCH override, and src/config.py sets BATCH_SIZE = int(os.environ.get("ARASL_BATCH", 32)) (train_model_g.py notes "Batch stays 32"). Together with F's epoch time being within 5% of C's on the same network (236 s against 226 s), everything agrees. The 761/761 steps in train_G.log belong to G's auxiliary classifier (batch 64), not the GAN.

6. Regressor training time done

Original run: at most 48 s on the RTX 4060. Windows keeps a creation time for each file. In Model B's first launch (driver.log: START B 19:46:59 on 2026-07-06), checkpoints/classes.npy was created at 19:49:49, right after image loading, and landmark_regressor.weights.h5 at 19:50:37, written once after training. Everything in between (loading the cached landmarks, building the models and the 20 regressor epochs) took 48 s.

Re-timed on the same GPU: 42.9 s. The same train_regressor() was run again on the training PC on 2026-11-08 (new weights path, originals untouched): 20 epochs in 42.9 s, which fits inside the 48 s window. Suggested wording: "the regressor trains in under a minute (about 43 s) on the RTX 4060".

Files: training_machine/extra.txt, training_machine/regressor_gpu_retime.log, landmark_regressor_eval_gpu_retime.json. The re-trained copy's error (10.9 px) differs from the original's (9.18 px) because GPU training is not bit-deterministic; the manuscript should use the original weights' numbers from item 5.

Earlier evidence, kept for reference:

9. Qualitative description of the visual differences done

From viewing real_vs_generated.png and the five samples_*.png grids:

  • Models A and B (noise only). Outputs are hand-like, with recognisable pose for open-hand letters such as al, bb, la and sheen. Fingers are soft and often merged or missing. Closed-fist letters (fa, gaaf, ha, khaa) collapse into fragmentary dark masses. Backgrounds are uniformly light. Pose varies widely between samples of the same letter. B shows no visible improvement over A.
  • Model C. Outputs follow the reference hand's silhouette with sharp contours. Periodic checkerboard artefacts appear in the background: scattered patches on light backgrounds, and across the whole frame on dark or textured ones (dha, ghain, ha, khaa, laam, thal).
  • Model F. Same structure as C. The artefacts are largely gone on light backgrounds but remain on dark or textured ones.
  • Model G. The cleanest of the five. Only faint residual speckle remains on a few letters (ha, khaa, laam, thal, aleff).
  • C, F and G together. The eight samples per letter are visually near-identical. The noise vector has little visible effect, so variety comes from the structure map, not from the noise.
