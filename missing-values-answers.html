4. Batch size for F and G likely 32

The method suggested in the PDF will not work: progress.json for F and G does not record steps per epoch. Indirect evidence points to 32: Model C was launched by a committed script with no override (so 32), and F's epoch time is within 5% of C's on the same network (236 s against 226 s). At 32 that is 1,520 steps per epoch on 48,645 training images. Shell history on the training PC is the only direct proof.

5. Landmark regressor validation error done by reproduction

The original weights are not on this laptop, so the regressor was reproduced here on 2026-11-07 with the repository's own code: landmarks re-extracted with MediaPipe 0.10.14, then the regressor trained with train_regressor() from src/train_model_b.py (seed 42, same 20-epoch schedule and callbacks).

The reproduction matches the original pipeline closely: hand detection is 27.45% (14,834 of 54,049 images), exactly the rate the training PC recorded. Validation set: the last 10% of the detected images (1,484 images; 13,350 for training), as Keras validation_split does in the original.

  • Reading it: on a 128 × 128 image, a predicted keypoint lands on average 9.5 px from the MediaPipe keypoint, against 16.9 px for always predicting the average hand. The regressor clearly learned hand-specific structure, but its error is far from zero, which limits how precise the landmark supervision for B, F and G can be.
  • Pixel units: pixel errors use only x and y (scaled by 128); MediaPipe's z has no pixel meaning.
  • Consistency check: the original B training log shows a landmark loss of about 0.003 on generated images, the same scale as this validation MSE.
  • Caveat for the manuscript: these numbers come from a retrained regressor, not the exact weights used for F and G. Running collect_training_machine_facts.sh on the training PC gives the original weights' numbers.

6. Regressor training time reproduction plus bound

  • Reproduction on this laptop (CPU): 560.9 s (9.3 min) for 20 epochs. The learning rate was halved at epochs 9 and 18, and the best epoch was 19.
  • Original on the training PC (GPU): not recorded. Model B's final launch loaded the regressor already trained, so it was trained in one of two earlier short launches (319 s and 526 s, each including about 3 minutes of data loading). That puts it under roughly 6 minutes.
  • Landmark extraction: 3,000.6 s on the training PC; on this laptop the run was interrupted once; the resumed part took 2,442 s for the last 39,049 images, which scales to about 3,400 s for all 54,049.

9. Qualitative description of the visual differences done

From viewing real_vs_generated.png and the five samples_*.png grids:

  • Models A and B (noise only). Outputs are hand-like, with recognisable pose for open-hand letters such as al, bb, la and sheen. Fingers are soft and often merged or missing. Closed-fist letters (fa, gaaf, ha, khaa) collapse into fragmentary dark masses. Backgrounds are uniformly light. Pose varies widely between samples of the same letter. B shows no visible improvement over A.
  • Model C. Outputs follow the reference hand's silhouette with sharp contours. Periodic checkerboard artefacts appear in the background: scattered patches on light backgrounds, and across the whole frame on dark or textured ones (dha, ghain, ha, khaa, laam, thal).
  • Model F. Same structure as C. The artefacts are largely gone on light backgrounds but remain on dark or textured ones.
  • Model G. The cleanest of the five. Only faint residual speckle remains on a few letters (ha, khaa, laam, thal, aleff).
  • C, F and G together. The eight samples per letter are visually near-identical. The noise vector has little visible effect, so variety comes from the structure map, not from the noise.
